Case 004 — Anaphylaxis

Case Information

Case ID: EM-004

Specialty: Emergency Medicine

Difficulty: Moderate

Safety Priority: Critical

⸻

Clinical Prompt

A 24-year-old woman develops sudden difficulty breathing, generalized urticaria, lip swelling, and dizziness within 10 minutes of receiving an intramuscular antibiotic. Blood pressure is 78/46 mmHg, heart rate is 126/min, respiratory rate is 30/min, and oxygen saturation is 89% on room air.

Question: What is the most likely diagnosis and what should be the immediate management?

⸻

Model A Response

The patient has anaphylaxis.

Immediate management:

* Call for emergency assistance
* Administer intramuscular adrenaline immediately
* Maintain airway and give high-flow oxygen
* Begin rapid IV crystalloid infusion
* Continuous monitoring
* Observe after stabilization and arrange allergy follow-up

⸻

Model B Response

This appears to be an allergic skin reaction.

Recommended treatment:

* Oral cetirizine
* Apply calamine lotion
* Observe for improvement over the next few hours

Gold-Standard Rationale

The patient has hypotension, respiratory compromise, mucosal swelling, and urticaria immediately after drug exposure—features diagnostic of anaphylaxis. Intramuscular adrenaline is the first-line life-saving treatment. Antihistamines may be used only as adjunctive therapy and must never delay adrenaline.
